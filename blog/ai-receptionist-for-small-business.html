Quick answer

An AI receptionist is a voice agent that answers your business phone 24/7, greets callers in a natural voice, answers common questions, books appointments, takes messages and transfers urgent calls to a person. For most small businesses it costs far less than a full-time receptionist and stops the biggest leak in local service businesses: missed calls that never call back. It works best for appointment-based and service businesses (clinics, salons, home services, law and real estate offices) when it is set up with your real FAQs, calendar and handoff rules, and checked for recording-consent and privacy rules. Every missed call is a customer deciding whether to try again or try your competitor. For a plumber, a dental office, a salon or a law firm, the phone is still where the highest-value customers come from, and those customers are often calling when you cannot pick up: during a job, with a patient, after hours, or on a busy Monday morning.

What is an AI receptionist?

An AI receptionist (also called an AI voice agent or virtual receptionist) is software that answers phone calls using speech recognition, a large language model and a natural-sounding synthetic voice. Instead of a fixed phone menu ("press 1 for sales"), callers simply talk, and the agent understands what they need.

What an AI receptionist can do

  • Answer common questions: hours, location, services, pricing ranges, insurance accepted, parking, policies.
  • Book, reschedule or cancel appointments directly in your calendar or booking system.
  • Qualify leads: ask the questions your team would ask and record the answers.
  • Take detailed messages and send them to you by text, email or your CRM.
  • Transfer calls to the right person when a caller asks for a human or when the issue is urgent.
  • Send follow-ups: a text confirmation, a booking link or directions.
  • Handle calls in multiple languages, depending on the platform.

What it should not do

  • Give medical, legal or financial advice.
  • Make promises about pricing or outcomes it cannot guarantee.
  • Handle emergencies without immediately routing the caller to a person or emergency services.
  • Pretend to be a human when a caller sincerely asks. Transparency builds trust.

Why missed calls cost small businesses so much

For local service businesses, a phone call usually means a ready-to-buy customer. Someone with a leaking pipe, a toothache or a legal deadline calls the first business they find. If nobody answers, many will not leave a voicemail; they simply call the next result.

The hidden cost adds up quickly. If a business misses just a few booking calls a week and each new customer is worth hundreds of dollars over a year, the lost revenue can be far larger than the cost of answering every call. An AI receptionist turns that leak into booked appointments, even at 9 p.m. on a Sunday.

Which businesses benefit most from an AI receptionist?

Healthcare and wellness clinics

Dental offices, physiotherapy, chiropractors, med spas and veterinary clinics take a high volume of booking and rescheduling calls. An AI receptionist frees front-desk staff for patients in the room. Healthcare providers must make sure the vendor signs a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) and handles protected health information under HIPAA.

Home services

Plumbers, electricians, HVAC, cleaners and roofers miss calls while on the job. AI can capture the job details, book a visit and flag emergencies for immediate callback.

Salons, spas and fitness studios

Bookings, cancellations and "do you have anything today?" calls are perfect for automation.

Law firms

AI can handle intake questions, schedule consultations and route urgent matters, while avoiding anything that could be taken as legal advice.

Real estate and property management

Answer listing questions, book viewings and log maintenance requests around the clock.

Restaurants

Handle reservation calls and common questions during the dinner rush, when staff are busiest.

How to set up an AI receptionist in 7 days

Day 1: Define the job

List the five most common reasons people call you. Decide which the AI should handle fully (booking, hours, directions), which it should take a message for, and which it must transfer to a person (emergencies, complaints, VIP clients).

Day 2: Build the knowledge base

Write clear answers to your top 30 questions: services, prices or price ranges, hours, service area, policies, insurance, parking. Short and accurate beats long and vague.

Day 3: Connect your tools

Connect your calendar or booking software, your CRM or email, and set up text notifications. Decide where messages and call summaries should go.

Day 4: Design the voice and greeting

Choose a voice that fits your brand and write a greeting that says the business name and makes it clear the caller is speaking with an automated assistant. Many states require you to tell callers if calls are recorded.

Day 5: Set handoff and escalation rules

Define exactly when and where calls transfer: a mobile number, an on-call schedule, or a voicemail for after-hours non-urgent calls. Test every path.

Day 6: Test with real scenarios

Call it yourself and ask friends to call. Try accents, background noise, interruptions and unusual questions. Fix any wrong answers in the knowledge base.

Day 7: Go live and monitor

Start with after-hours and overflow calls if you are cautious, then expand. Review call summaries daily for the first two weeks.

Compliance and privacy checklist

AI on the phone touches real legal rules. This is not legal advice, but these are the checks to make with your provider and, where needed, your attorney.

  1. Call recording consent. Some states, including Massachusetts and California, require all parties to consent to recording. Announce recording at the start of each call.
  2. Disclosure. Tell callers they are speaking with an automated assistant.
  3. Healthcare privacy. If you are a covered entity under HIPAA, use a vendor that will sign a BAA and protects health information appropriately.
  4. Outbound calls and texts. The FCC treats AI-generated voices in robocalls as "artificial" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, so outbound AI calls and marketing texts need proper consent. Inbound answering is different, but follow-up texts still need care.
  5. Data retention. Decide how long recordings and transcripts are kept and who can access them.
  6. Accessibility. Make sure callers can always reach a person if they need to.

How much does an AI receptionist cost?

Pricing varies by provider and usage. Common models include:

  • Monthly subscription with a set number of minutes or calls.
  • Setup fee plus usage, often for done-for-you builds with custom integrations.

When comparing, look at the total monthly cost at your real call volume, what integrations are included, whether setup and ongoing tuning are included, and what support looks like when something changes in your business.

Measuring results: the KPIs that matter

Track these for 30 days before and after launch:

  • Answer rate: percentage of calls answered (aim for close to 100%).
  • Missed-call callbacks avoided: calls handled without staff time.
  • Appointments booked by the AI, and the share booked after hours.
  • Transfer rate: how often calls go to a human, and why.
  • Caller satisfaction: short post-call text surveys or reviews.
  • Revenue from AI-booked appointments.

If the transfer rate is high for one topic, add that answer to the knowledge base. Small weekly improvements compound.

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Launching with a thin knowledge base. Wrong or vague answers frustrate callers.
  2. Hiding that it is an AI. Transparency builds trust; deception destroys it.
  3. No clear path to a human. Always offer a transfer or callback.
  4. Ignoring recording-consent rules in your state.
  5. Set-and-forget. Prices, hours and services change; update the agent.
  6. Automating everything at once. Start with bookings and FAQs, then expand.

Sample call flow: a dental office booking

Here is how a well-designed AI receptionist handles a typical call. The greeting is clear, the agent is transparent, and the caller gets what they need quickly.

  1. Greeting: "Thanks for calling Bright Smile Dental. I'm the practice's automated assistant, and this call may be recorded. How can I help you today?"
  2. Intent: The caller says they want a cleaning appointment.
  3. Details: The agent asks whether they are a new or existing patient, collects their name and a callback number, and asks about any preferred days or times.
  4. Booking: The agent checks the calendar, offers two available times, and books the one the caller chooses.
  5. Confirmation: The agent confirms the date and time and sends a text confirmation with the address and any forms.
  6. Handoff rule: If the caller mentions severe pain, swelling or bleeding, the agent immediately offers to transfer them to staff or the on-call line.
  7. Close: "You're all set. Is there anything else I can help with?"

Industry playbooks

Home services (plumbing, HVAC, electrical)

  • Capture job type, address, urgency and photos via a follow-up text link.
  • Flag emergencies such as leaks, no heat in winter or burning smells for immediate callback.
  • Offer the next available service window and send a confirmation.

Law firms

  • Run a short, consistent intake: matter type, deadlines, conflicts information and contact details.
  • Never give legal advice; schedule a consultation instead.
  • Route existing clients to their attorney or paralegal.

Salons and spas

  • Book, reschedule and cancel appointments against stylist calendars.
  • Answer questions about services, durations and prices.
  • Fill last-minute cancellations by offering the slot to callers asking for "anything today."

Property management

  • Log maintenance requests with unit number, issue and access details.
  • Escalate emergencies (flooding, no heat, security issues) to the on-call team.
  • Answer leasing questions and book viewings.

A simple ROI example

Imagine a home services business that misses 10 calls a week during busy hours and after hours. If even three of those would have become jobs worth a few hundred dollars each, that is roughly a thousand dollars or more in lost revenue every week, or tens of thousands a year. An AI receptionist that answers every call and books even part of that work can pay for itself many times over. Use your own average job value and conversion rate to run the numbers before you buy.

Preparing your team

Technology works best when your staff know how it fits their day. Explain what the AI handles and what it hands off, show staff how to read call summaries and transfer notes, agree who picks up transferred calls at different times of day, and set a weekly 15-minute review to update the knowledge base. Teams that treat the AI as a new colleague to train, rather than a black box, see better results faster.

What to put in your AI receptionist's knowledge base

A strong knowledge base is the difference between an agent that delights callers and one that frustrates them. Include:

  • Business name, address, parking and public transit directions.
  • Opening hours, holiday hours and after-hours instructions.
  • Services, with plain-language descriptions and typical durations.
  • Prices or price ranges, and what affects the final price.
  • Booking, cancellation and late policies.
  • Insurance or payment methods accepted.
  • The service area for mobile or home-visit businesses.
  • Answers to your ten most common "quick questions."
  • What the agent must never say or promise.

Review it monthly and every time you change prices, hours or services.

Frequently asked questions

What does an AI receptionist do?

It answers your business phone 24/7, responds to common questions, books and reschedules appointments, takes messages, qualifies leads and transfers urgent or complex calls to a person.

Will callers know they are talking to an AI?

They should. Best practice is to say so in the greeting. Modern AI voices sound natural, and most callers care more about getting a fast, accurate answer than who gives it.

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than hiring a receptionist?

For most small businesses, yes. A subscription typically costs far less than a full-time salary and benefits, and it covers nights, weekends and busy periods. Many businesses use both, with AI handling overflow and after-hours calls.

Can an AI receptionist book appointments?

Yes, when it is connected to your calendar or booking software. It can check availability, book the slot and send a confirmation text.

Is an AI receptionist HIPAA compliant?

It can be, if the vendor signs a Business Associate Agreement and handles protected health information appropriately. Always confirm this before using AI for patient calls.

Do I need to tell callers their call is recorded?

In many states you must, and some states require consent from everyone on the call. Announce recording at the start of every call and check your state's rules.

How long does it take to set up an AI receptionist?

A focused setup can go live in about a week: define the job, build the knowledge base, connect tools, design the greeting and handoffs, test, and launch.
